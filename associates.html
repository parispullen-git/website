File 006 · Known Associates

The
Associates

One man gets photographed. Six people make it work. None of them have a public title, none of them want one, and every single one of them can tell him no.

They are known by function. That is not an affectation — it is how he actually refers to them, and after a while it is how they refer to themselves.

All six are under forty. All six could pass for the client rather than the staff, which is the point: in this building nobody can tell by looking who works here and who is being handled.

Nobody is put in a uniform. Each of them has a wholly separate sartorial personality — tonal flannel, chalk stripe, glen check, windowpane, pinstripe — and the only shared rules are that the tailoring is classic, the pattern is chosen rather than defaulted to, and nothing carries a visible label. Six people who look like they share a tailor and nothing else.

The loudest man in the room is rarely the one who decides what happens in it.

Paris is the one who gets photographed, and he is entirely comfortable saying that the arrangement is deliberate. A face is useful. A face is also disposable. Six people who can each say no are considerably harder to replace.
