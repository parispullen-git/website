# PARISPULLEN.COM — The Gentleman Operator

A cinematic digital world for Paris Pullen. Not a portfolio, not a conventional
personal-brand site: the visitor enters a world and moves through it.

> The city thinks he's selling luxury.
> The people who matter know he's selling access.

Static HTML/CSS/JS. No build step, no framework, no dependencies. Upload and go.

---

## Pages

The site reads as an **open case file**. A visitor is not browsing a brand — they are
working an investigation into how one man assembled an empire in Charlotte.

### The narrative spine

Each move exists to solve a problem created by the one before it, and each carries a
one-line punchline in `.chrono__because`:

| # | Move | Because |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | The Scent | a bottle costs less than a storefront and travels further |
| 02 | The Cut | you cannot sell access wearing somebody else's uniform |
| 03 | The Room | a room is the only product that appreciates while you sleep |
| 04 | The Record | whoever keeps the record decides what the room meant |
| 05 | The House | renting the venue means somebody else holds the guest list |
| 06 | The Order | the building was never the asset — the list was |

Colour is deliberately scarce. Brass is the only accent that appears regularly;
everything else is a value, not a hue.

### Typography

- **Display** — Playfair Display. High-contrast serif for editorial headlines. `line-height: .92`, negative tracking.
- **Sans** — Inter. Navigation, body, metadata. Weight 300–500 only.
- **Mono** — system monospace stack. Reserved *exclusively* for classified/intelligence microcopy (`.classified`). Never for body text.

Fluid scale from `--t-micro` (11px) to `--t-mega` (up to 12rem), all `clamp()`-based.

| Class | Role |
|---|---|
| `.eyebrow` | Small uppercase brass label, `.34em` tracking |
| `.display--mega/h1/h2/h3` | Editorial headlines |
| `.lede` | Opening paragraph, `--bone` |
| `.body` | Body copy, `--silver`, max 62ch |
| `.statement` | Short declarative line in display serif |
| `.classified` | Mono microcopy — classified moments only |
| `.stat__num` | Oversized numerals for statistics |

### The building (§25 of `world.css`)

`house.html` is **generated**, not hand-written. Floors live as a data structure in
`build_house.py` — edit that and re-run `python3 build_house.py`.

```python
dict(id="bar", lvl="02", name="The Bar", state="public",
     img="env-bar", grade="", focus="46% 54%",
     note="Low ceiling, low light, low voices…",
     arts=[("ice","The Ice","36%","64%","Cut from a single block…",[("Cut","Daily")])])
```

| Component | Class | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Floor scene | `.floor-scene` | Full-viewport room. `--focus` sets `object-position`, `--zoom` the scale. |
| Grades | `.grade--warm/noir/cold/dawn/amber` | Colour treatments that let one source image serve several rooms |
| Floor plate | `.floor-plate` | Level number, name, note, artifact count. Carries its own local backdrop wash so bright rooms stay bright everywhere except under the type. |
| Artifact | `.artifact` | Hotspot pinned with `--x`/`--y`. Pulsing brass dot, label on hover/focus. |
| Drawer | `.drawer` | Slides up from the floor. One panel at a time, Esc closes, and leaving the floor closes it automatically. |
| Directory | `.directory` | The floor index, with public / members / restricted states |

**Artifact positioning is element-relative, not image-relative.** Because the images are
`object-fit: cover`, a hotspot drifts slightly against the underlying object as the
viewport aspect changes. Positions are therefore tuned as *regions of interest* rather
than pixel pins. If you need exact pinning later, compute the cover-crop mapping in JS
and set the hotspots from that.

### Reuse as narrative

Holding to the credit cap meant most floors reuse existing imagery under different
crops and grades. In two places that constraint became the story rather than a
compromise:

- **The Bar (L02) and The Coffee House (L01) are the same photograph.** Night grade
  versus dawn grade, different crop. The copy leans in: *"the same marble counter,
  twelve hours earlier."*
- **The Haberdashery (L12) and The Armoury (L12M)** are one room, the armoury being a
  hard `grade--noir` crop of the far corner — which is exactly what a hidden room
  behind a panel should look like.

### The Foxx mark (§4c–4e of `world.css`)

The fox is the **house mark** — it belongs to Foxx and sits above Bespoke, Motor Club,
the Network and the hotel. The Compliment is a property underneath it and takes a
wordmark endorsed by the fox, not a competing emblem of its own.

The mark lives at **`assets/img/foxx-mark.png`** — the real artwork, cropped from
`iMessages-Foxx.PNG` (white fox on black) and converted so its brightness becomes the
alpha channel. Replace that one file, keeping the filename, and it updates everywhere —
nav, gate and every footer seal. Intrinsic size 814 × 1180; `.foxx` sets
`aspect-ratio: 814/1180` to match, so change both together if you re-cut it.

**It is mounted as a CSS `mask`, not an `<img>`.** That means a plain black-on-white
silhouette works as-is and gets recoloured to brass on obsidian automatically. You never
need to produce an inverted version, and colour is controlled from CSS:

```css
.foxx { --foxx-color: var(--brass-lit); --foxx-size: 28px; }
```

Any solid silhouette works — SVG preferred, transparent PNG fine. Portrait proportions
around 3:4 match the master artwork.

| Component | Class | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Bare mark | `.foxx` | Nav (white, 20px), gate (brass, 54px) |
| Seal | `.seal` | Every page footer. Fine brass ring with **twelve tick positions, one deliberately empty** — the twelfth chair, hidden in plain sight. |
| Lockup | `.lockup` | Mark + rule + wordmark, stacked or inline (`.lockup--row`) |

**Small-size note.** The master artwork's serrated tail tip will clog below roughly
16mm. If the nav mark looks muddy once the real file is in, supply a simplified
small-size cut as `foxx-mark-sm.svg` and point `.worldnav__mark .foxx` at it.

### Motion

All motion is slow and eased with `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)`.

- `.reveal` + `.reveal-d1/d2/d3` — IntersectionObserver fade-and-rise with stagger
- `[data-parallax="0.10"]` — scroll parallax on scene media, rAF-throttled
- `.grain` / `.vignette` — fixed film atmosphere overlays
- Every animation collapses under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`

### Accessibility

- Semantic landmarks, one `<h1>` per page, logical heading order
- Visible `:focus-visible` ring in brass on every interactive element
- Secret doors and loadouts are keyboard operable with `aria-expanded` / `aria-controls`
- Sound defaults **off** and never autoplays; toggle exposes `aria-pressed`
- Reduced-motion honoured throughout, including skipping the gate

The client supplied four photographs of real people as facial references. **They were
never uploaded to OpenArt**, so these were generated from written feature-and-complexion
descriptions read off the photographs — complexion and undertone, hairline and cut,
beard shape, eye colour, face and jaw geometry — rather than true image-to-image. If
closer likeness is wanted later, upload the photos via the OpenArt picker and re-run
these four in `image2image` mode against them.

**Styling doctrine:** nobody wears a uniform. Each associate has a separate sartorial
personality, all classic tailoring, all different pattern language — tonal charcoal
flannel (Cutter), black pinstripe (Concierge), cream windowpane (Nose), navy chalk
stripe (Editor), glen check one-piece coverall with a black silk bow tie (Curator),
forest green faint check (Archivist).

The Curator needed two passes: the first rendered a two-piece suit. The retry names the
garment explicitly — one continuous piece, centre zip, waist seam joining top to
trousers — which produced a true tailored coverall.

The hero video (`hero-ballroom.mp4`) is client-supplied live action and cost nothing.

**Every Paris generation referenced the uploaded character sheet** (OpenArt upload
`tU0VcefkjHaXk2cuh08t`) via `image2image`, with the mandated identity-preservation
prefix, and was checked against the sheet before use.

The associates are **new characters**, so they were generated text-only in a
deliberately consistent register: modern crime-caper ensemble portraiture, anamorphic
feel, warm raking light, characterful faces, no glamour gloss.

Every prompt carried an explicit **TEXT RULE** forbidding invented brand names,
plaques, signage and labels. On the UR Welcome bottle the rule permitted exactly one
phrase — `UR WELCOME`. On the perfumer's bench and the editorial wall it forced
unlabelled bottles and blank pages, which is where these models otherwise invent
brands unprompted.

### Live-action footage

The hero on `index.html` is **client-supplied live-action footage** (`hero-ballroom.mp4`,
1920×1080, 4.96s, 4.3MB) — a black-tie ballroom with Paris centre-frame. It is not
AI-generated and cost no credits.

It arrives considerably brighter and cooler than the site palette, with teal daylight in
the background windows. Rather than re-encode it (no `ffmpeg` on this machine), it is
graded **in CSS** via `.scene__media--graded`, which applies to any live footage dropped
into a scene:

```css
filter: saturate(.62) contrast(1.16) brightness(.79) sepia(.17) hue-rotate(-8deg);
```

plus an `--espresso` layer at `mix-blend-mode: color` and a soft brass bloom. This kills
the stray cyan, warms everything toward brass and crushes the blacks toward obsidian,
without touching the source file. Scrims and grain still sit on top untouched.

To swap in different footage, drop it in `assets/video/`, point the hero `<source>` at it,
and the same grade applies automatically. Adjust the filter values if the new footage is
warmer or darker to begin with.

The poster (`hero-ballroom.jpg`) is a frame pulled from the same video with `qlmanage`,
so the still and the motion match exactly. Video loading is skipped entirely when the
browser reports `saveData` or a 2G connection — the poster then holds, which reads as
intentional rather than broken.

`hero-pushin.mp4`, the AI-generated push-in that previously ran here, is retired to
`../paris-pullen-masters/` rather than deleted.

### The technology brief

The ops room and the carry set were written to one constraint: *if a stranger can tell
it is technology, it has failed.* Warm amber and brass only — every prompt explicitly
forbade blue, cyan, neon, holograms and sci-fi glow. The result is a walnut table with
amber wireframe maps inlaid under smoked glass, lit by brass task lamps. Tony Stark
by way of a Charlotte library, not a spaceship.

Full-resolution PNG masters live outside the web root in `../paris-pullen-masters/`
so they are never deployed. Web images are JPEG at two widths each
(`name.jpg`, `name@sm.jpg`) wired through `srcset`.

### Regenerating derivatives

```bash
sips -Z 2400 -s format jpeg -s formatOptions 68 master.png --out out.jpg
```

If you install `cwebp`/`avifenc` later, add `<picture>` sources — the markup is
ready for it.

## What is real and what is world-building

Paris Pullen, Charlotte, the Heineken 100 honour, the 36-city hosting tour, the brand
activation work and UR Welcome are real.

**The Compliment, Foxx Bespoke, Foxx Motor Club and The Order are fiction** — the
cinematic architecture of an ongoing creative world. This is stated in the site footer.

**The six associates are composite characters.** They are named by function — The
Cutter, The Concierge, The Nose, The Editor, The Curator, The Archivist — rather than
given invented personal names, precisely so they cannot be mistaken for real named
staff. The associates page states this outright: *the roles are real, the people are
written.*

The secret-society framing — the unlisted floor, the twelve chairs, the sealed
findings — is a storytelling device about access, relationships and influence. Nothing
on this site depicts criminal activity, and the "investigation" is a narrative frame,
not a claim that anyone is under investigation.

## The superseded bundle

`_superseded/old-bundle/` holds the previous build — an 8.4MB compiled React bundle
(`paris-pullen-site-bundle.html`) plus eight images recovered from inside it.

It cannot be edited: the source is minified and gone. What it *does* contain is a
complete **commerce and service layer** this site does not have — booking, made to
measure, alterations, shop with sizes and sold-out states, house accounts, a members'
card, a waitlist. Its palette (`#0C0C0E`, `#17181B`, `#191A1D`) is within a shade of
ours, so the two are visually compatible.

Two lines of its copy were good enough to keep and are recorded in the voice section
of the design system:

> Your measurements are held under a record number, not your name.
> Nothing is charged today. The fee is settled at the fitting.

The gaps table in `design-system.html` tracks what still needs building from it.
